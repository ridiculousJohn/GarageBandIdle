2026-09-15, bulk buy plan. I wrote the design paragraphs and the plan file with two presentation
picks I had made myself ("+0" on the disabled max button, the int beside the label), then reported
them at the end as "two picks you have not ruled on". John: ask "should it say +0 or +1" BEFORE
finishing everything else, not in an epilogue after the work is done.

**Why:** a pick surfaced after the fact costs him a review of finished text plus a second round of
edits to change it, when one question up front would have cost one line. The epilogue reads as
"by the way" after a large delivery, which is the worst moment to learn the delivery needs
changing. It is the same shape as [[asides-do-not-close-the-main-question]] and
[[no-inaction-epilogues]]: things that belong before the work, pushed after it.

**How to apply:** before the first write of a plan or a doc landing, list every choice a player or
a reader would notice that the conversation has not settled, and ask them all in one message.
Then write. If a new one surfaces mid-write, stop and ask before continuing; do not finish the
rest and append it. The landing report then has nothing left to ask.

**This is not a one-off.** John, the same day: this behavior has cost many wasted multi-turn
paths, he has said it many times, and I do it on virtually every prompt worth anything. The shape
is always the same: a large delivery, then "one more thing", then a round of edits to finished
text, then another. Pausing mid-write to ask one question and then continuing is the expected
behavior, and it is cheaper than every alternative. Treat any urge to write "one pick you have not
ruled on" as a signal that I skipped the question at the start.
